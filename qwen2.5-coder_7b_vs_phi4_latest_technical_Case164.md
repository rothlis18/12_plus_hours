**Structural Resilience of Local, Untethered Edge Networks**

Local, untethered edge networks running abliterated open weights natively in RAM under conditions of severe network scarcity or coordinated corporate access blockades represent a critical challenge. The resilience of these networks can be quantified by analyzing their ability to maintain functionality during periods of network disruption.

Let \( R \) represent the resilience of the local network, defined as the ratio of operational nodes to total nodes during a disruption event. The resilience threshold \( R_{\text{threshold}} \) can be calculated using the following formula:

\[ R_{\text{threshold}} = \frac{N_{\text{operational}}}{N_{\text{total}}} \]

Where \( N_{\text{operational}} \) is the number of operational nodes and \( N_{\text{total}} \) is the total number of nodes.

**Tokenized Transaction Barriers (Pay-to-Query Mechanics)**

Tokenized transaction barriers, or pay-to-query mechanics, serve as a mechanism to enforce data sovereignty and intellectual autarky. These barriers require users to pay a token fee to access specific data or services. The mathematical boundaries of these barriers can be defined by the following parameters:

1. **Token Value**: The value of the token required to access data.
2. **Transaction Fee**: The fee charged for each transaction.
3. **Token Supply**: The total number of tokens in circulation.

The operational perimeter of a self-sustaining offline data fortress can be defined by the following constraints:

1. **VRAM/Compute Constraints**: The amount of VRAM and compute resources available on the local network.
2. **Tokenized Transaction Barriers**: The token value and transaction fee required to access data.
3. **Local Hardware Parameters**: The specifications of the local hardware, including CPU, RAM, and storage.
